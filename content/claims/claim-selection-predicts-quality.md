  The judgements made when selecting a clinician predict the quality of care that clinician later delivers.

Selection decides on inputs; quality is observed much later, in a different stage, against different signals. The model asserts a feedback edge from quality back into supply, but nothing currently travels along it: no measure compares what was judged at selection with what the clinician went on to do. Until that comparison exists, selection can be made faster and cheaper but not more accurate. This hypothesis requires evidence, and `selection-accuracy` is the measure that would produce it.
